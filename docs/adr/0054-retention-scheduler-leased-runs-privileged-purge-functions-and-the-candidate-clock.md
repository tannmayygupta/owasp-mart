# 0054. Retention: a scheduler with leased runs, two privileged purge functions, and the candidate's clock

- **Status:** Proposed (the builder's design for the developer to confirm; the requirements fix the numbers, OI-33's legal review is still open)
- **Date:** 2026-10-19
- **Deciders:** Claude Code (design); the developer to confirm
- **Requirements:** FR-PRV-04, FR-PRV-05, FR-PRV-06, FR-PRV-07, FR-PRV-14, FR-PRV-15, FR-PRV-16, FR-PRV-17, FR-PRV-18, OI-37, ADR 0047, 0050, 0051, 0052
- **Handoffs:** H-144

## Context
The platform keeps a candidate's assessment (the lab's attempt data, the invite, the consent record), a learner's account and practice data, sessions, mail, the audit log and the live-update outbox. The requirements give the times (candidate: the template's 30 to 365 days, default 180, from the end of the attempt or the invite date; learner: 12 months after the last login, a warning 30 days before; evidence: 30 days after the instance was destroyed; IP and browser details: 90 days; security and system logs: 12 months; the short consent record outlives the assessment record, OI-37) and want a scheduled job for each, with failures shown to the admins and the last run visible. Three things had to be decided: how jobs run when several API processes exist, how the append-only audit log and consent records can be purged at all when the application role may not delete from them, and which clock starts when.

## Options considered
1. **Where jobs run:** the worker process / the API process; one process only / any process with a lease.
2. **Purging append-only tables:** give the application role DELETE / a function owned by the table owner that does exactly one thing.
3. **When a candidate's record is due:** the invite date for everything / the end of the attempt, or the invite date when the candidate never started.
4. **A learner who is also a candidate:** delete the account / keep it until the assessment data is gone.
5. **A failure:** an email to the admins / a recorded run, an audit entry and a log line that the admin area shows.

## Decision
- **Scheduler** (`privacy/retention/scheduler.py`, `privacy/retention/build.py`): a job is a name, an interval and a function that returns a short fixed summary. Eleven jobs: invite expiry (hourly), invite token purge, candidate retention, consent purge, learner warning, learner retention, evidence retention, account housekeeping, log purge, audit anchor (daily), live cleanup (hourly). A job is due when its last success is older than its interval, or ten minutes after a failure (never sooner, however overdue). A run is claimed by inserting a `running` row in a transaction that holds a Postgres advisory lock, and only when no run of that job is `running` and younger than an hour, so any number of API processes can run the scheduler and one runs each job; a run that died is written off as failed by the next scheduler that sees it. The scheduler runs in the **API process** (setting `scheduler_enabled`, on in Compose): that is where the lab runtime lives, and the lab's erase (key shredded, rows deleted, attempt marked purged) is the only safe way to remove an attempt (`LabRuntime.erase`); a job that needs it and has none fails visibly instead of deleting around it.
- **Reporting a failure** (FR-PRV-18): the run stays in `job_runs` with the class of the exception (never its text), an audit entry `job.failed` names the job, and an error is logged; `Scheduler.status` is what the admin dashboard reads (the last run, the last success and the next due time of every job; the route belongs to the admin area, P-28). No email to the admins: FR-NTF-01 lists the email types and who gets an incident alert is OI-12 and OI-34.
- **Candidate clock** (`retention/candidates.py`): due at the end of the attempt plus the template's days (copied onto the invite; 180 by default), or, when the candidate never started, at the invite date plus those days; an attempt that is still going is never due; an attempt whose row is already gone makes the invite due at once. When due, in this order and each step safe to repeat: the lab erases the attempt, the consent records of the attempt are scheduled to go `consent_record_extra_months` (12) later, the invite is deleted. The audit entry names the company and counts, never a person.
- **Two privileged functions** (migration 0014, `SECURITY DEFINER`, executable by the application role only): `purge_audit_log(before)` deletes the audit rows older than `before` but never younger than 12 months and never the newest entry, after writing the checkpoint (the hash of the last purged row), so the chain verifies from the checkpoint and the next entry chains to the newest; `purge_consent_records()` deletes the consent records whose `erase_after` has passed and nothing else. The application role may set `consent_records.erase_after` (one column) and still cannot update or delete anything else of a record or of the audit log. It also gets DELETE on `invites` (row-level security still applies) and on `security_events` (insert-only in the lab's migration; the logs are purged after 12 months).
- **Learners** (`retention/learners.py`): a verified, active learner whose deletion is 30 days away is sent the warning (`learner.retention_warning`; the mail and `users.retention_warned_at` are one transaction); a login clears the mark, so a login cancels the deletion; a learner warned at least 30 days ago and still past the 12 months loses their practice attempts (the lab erases each) and their account (sessions, tokens, second factor and roles cascade) and their audit pseudonym (`subject_map`) is forgotten, so the log entries identify nobody. A learner who also took part in an assessment (a candidate attempt or a consent record exists) keeps the account until that data is gone by its own date. A learner who never logged in counts from the day the account was made. A year is 12 months of 365/12 days.
- **Housekeeping** (`retention/housekeeping.py`): sessions that ended 90 days ago are deleted with their network prefix and browser class (FR-PRV-14), used and expired email tokens 30 days after they expired; a learner's evidence is deleted 30 days after the instance was destroyed (the lab does not store the instant it finished; the claim to destroy, or the end of the attempt, is used); security events, finished job runs and sent mail rows after 12 months and the sealed address of a mail that never left after 30 days (a mail still waiting keeps it); published rows of the live-update outbox after a week and idle live streams.
- **What people are told** (FR-PRV-07, FR-PRV-16): the data-processing terms now also say VulnMart is the controller of a learner's practice data (version 2026-10-19.1; companies that accepted the earlier version accept the new one before they invite again); a test scans the consent notice, the terms and every email for any claim of a legal duty or a 48-hour notice and finds none.
- **Synthetic data only** (FR-PRV-17): `scripts/check-synthetic-data.mjs` (run by `node --test` in CI) scans the shop's seed folders for email addresses on domains that are not reserved for examples, numbers that pass the card check and the formats of well-known secrets; it cannot judge a real person's name.

## Consequences
- A job that fails is visible and tried again; one that is long overdue still waits ten minutes after a failure.
- With several API processes the lease means one runs a job; a dead run holds a job for up to an hour.
- The application role can write a checkpoint row (it always could: `audit_checkpoint` is insert-only for it), so the anchors kept outside the database remain what proves the log was not cut short.
- Candidate data is deleted by the platform when its time is up, and the company's own deletion and the candidate's request are story P-25.
- The learner rule deletes the account row; the company's candidate data of the same person is a separate clock.
- `retention_days` of a template cannot be changed for an invite already sent (ADR 0050), so a later change of a template's days does not move an invite's date.
- Mail rows older than 12 months are purged even though FR-PRV-15 names security and system logs only; the outbox is the delivery log.
